**Results & Discussion**

Most adjusters performed consistently across the classification tasks. Top-performing simple transformations—such as genewise Mean-Variance Matching (Naive) and Rank Twice—demonstrated little variability and few interactions. However, supervised ComBat adjustment showed a particularly severe negative interaction with one classifier family: $k$-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) (Figure 1). Linear and kernel classifiers (shrinkage LDA, SVM, elastic net) were unaffected or slightly improved; only the distance-based classifier collapsed, falling below chance (MCC $0.63 \rightarrow -0.19$).

This selectivity is the first clue to the mechanism. The interaction is not an empirical artifact—it is a predictable geometric consequence of how ComBat estimates *variance* under cross-study confounding, and of which classifiers read raw geometry at face value. KNN relies strictly on the preservation of absolute Euclidean neighborhoods; LDA and SVM whiten or re-weight the feature space ($S_W^{-1}$) and so absorb the same distortion. The damage is therefore a distance-geometry problem, not a statistical-inference problem.

**The variance identity at the root.** ComBat's additive model assumes a single, globally shared biological effect for each gene ($\hat{\beta}_g$). To isolate technical batch parameters, the algorithm strips this estimated effect from the data and measures the residual variance. But when studies and classes are confounded, the true biological effect varies by batch ($\beta_{ig}$), leaving an unmodeled mismatch $\Delta\beta_{ig} = \beta_{ig} - \hat{\beta}_g$. The algorithm cannot distinguish this leftover biology from technical noise, so the within-batch residual variance it estimates is a *mixture* of the two:

$$s^2_{e, ig} = (\Delta\beta_{ig})^2\,\text{Var}(X_{ij}) + \sigma^2_{true, ig}$$

We confirmed this identity empirically across all 24 leave-one-study-out scenarios: ComBat's standardized batch scale, mapped back to the raw expression scale, reproduces $(\Delta\beta)^2\text{Var}(X) + \sigma^2_{true}$ essentially perfectly ($R^2 = 1.00$; Figure 2). The standardized batch-scale factor $\hat{\delta}^2_{ig}$ is this mixed variance divided by the pooled variance—so it is corrupted by biology whenever $\Delta\beta \neq 0$.

**One defect, two faces.** The corrupted scale factor distorts geometry in two opposite ways depending on a gene's biology:

- *Deflation and sign inversion* ($\hat{\delta}^2_{ig} < 1$). For genes whose biology matches the consensus ($\Delta\beta \approx 0$) and whose native technical noise is low, the mixed variance falls *below* the pooled average. ComBat then divides the residual by a fraction, **amplifying** each study's leftover class signal rather than shrinking it. Reconstruction yields a per-study corrected class gap of $\hat{\delta}^{-1}_{ig}(\text{gap}_{ig} - \hat{\beta}_g) + \hat{\beta}_g$; when $\hat{\delta} < 1$ this can overshoot zero and **flip the class axis**.
- *Inflation and erasure* ($\hat{\delta}^2_{ig} \gg 1$). For strongly differentially expressed genes, the $(\Delta\beta)^2$ term dominates, ComBat reads the unmodeled biology as massive noise, and divides the signal away—crushing the class difference.

These are two consequences of the same conflation. **In this data the deflation/inversion face dominates.** Roughly 60% of genes carry $\hat{\delta}^2 < 1$, and among genes in the flip zone 100% are low-noise and 93% are consensus-biology—exactly the deflation profile. The inflation face, though derivable, essentially does not occur here: of genes with compressed biology and low noise, none acquire $\hat{\delta}^2 > 1$, because the TB class effects are too small for the mismatch term to overpower the low noise. We note inflation explicitly because data with larger, more heterogeneous effects would exhibit the mirror failure.

**Why the inversion survives averaging: broken cancellation.** Individually, per-study disagreements about the class direction should cancel: the class-mix–weighted leftovers sum to zero, $\sum_i w_i(\text{gap}_{ig} - \hat{\beta}_g) = 0$. The variance step destroys this balance by dividing each study's leftover by its own $\sqrt{\hat{\delta}_{ig}}$ before they are combined. Heterogeneous, sub-unit $\hat{\delta}$ up-weights the low-noise studies, and the leftovers no longer cancel—they accumulate into a net residual that, in failing scenarios, is several times the protected class effect and points *against* it (Figure 3, Figure 4). The corrected reference axis becomes this residual. The Empirical Bayes shrinkage is not the trigger: the raw and EB-shrunk scale factors are nearly identical in failing scenarios, and the inversion occurs with or without shrinkage.

**Why it surfaces under projection, and worsens out-of-distribution.** The training samples are *moved together with* the distorted axis during the supervised joint fit, so they remain mutually consistent: resubstitution accuracy stays high. The damage emerges when samples are instead placed onto the finished reference by the label-blind step-2 projection, which positions them by overall density rather than by class. Two distinct contributions compound here. First, projection itself carries an intrinsic penalty independent of distribution shift: taking the *same* in-distribution samples—drawn from a study that helped build the reference but withheld from the fit—and placing them by step-2 instead of by the joint fit lowers KNN accuracy in 5 of 6 scenarios (mean MCC $0.71 \rightarrow 0.66$), bringing in-distribution samples down to roughly the level of a projected external study (Figure 5). Second, genuine distribution shift supplies the tail risk: an external study, whose true class axis need not align with the bent reference axis, is what tips a tax into a below-chance inversion (e.g., one held-out study at MCC $0.40$ while its own in-distribution samples, projected identically, survive at $0.81$). The operative variable is therefore whether a sample's coordinates come from the label-aware joint fit or from label-blind projection onto an axis bent by supervised variance scaling—with the severity set by how far the new study's biology departs from that axis.

**Solutions.** Safe harmonization for distance-based pipelines must decouple technical-scale estimation from biological-effect estimation. Two strategies achieve this, both restoring KNN to baseline (MCC $\approx 0.67$–$0.71$, 0/24 scenarios below chance):

1. *Mean-only correction* abandons variance scaling entirely ($\hat{\delta} \equiv 1$). This restores the cancellation exactly and is provably immune to inversion, at the cost of leaving genuine technical scale differences uncorrected.
2. *Class-isolated variance scaling* estimates scale strictly from within-class (or shared-control) variation, as in the COCONUT approach. Because the within-class variance excludes the between-class gap, the mismatch term $(\Delta\beta)^2\text{Var}(X)$ never enters the residual, quarantining the conflation at its source. This both fixes KNN and preserves each study's internal Cohen's $d$ exactly.

Notably, partial fixes that keep the supervised variance step but soften it—for example dividing by $\sqrt{\hat{\delta}}$ instead of $\hat{\delta}$—do *not* close the flip zone (median MCC $-0.23$, worse than uncorrected supervised ComBat). The variance step must be removed or sourced from class-isolated estimates; merely attenuating it is insufficient.

**The general lesson.** A supervised covariate does not make batch correction safe. Whenever a harmonizer estimates per-feature variance from residuals that still contain unmodeled biological heterogeneity, it will mis-scale the feature space—and distance-based learners are the ones that pay for it. "Preserves the class effect statistically" is not the same as "preserves the geometry a nearest-neighbor classifier needs," and the failure is invisible to validation schemes that do not project genuinely external samples onto the harmonized reference.
